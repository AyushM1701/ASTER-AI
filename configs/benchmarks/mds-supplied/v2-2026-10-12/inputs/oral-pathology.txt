Level 1 — Basic Oral Histology and Pathology
Tests foundational understanding and simple-versus-detailed explanations.
1. What is oral epithelium? Explain its different layers and functions.
2. Differentiate between keratinized and non-keratinized oral epithelium with examples.
3. Explain the structure, function, and histological differences between oral epithelium, sulcular epithelium, and junctional epithelium.
4. What is apoptosis? Explain its molecular mechanism and histological features.
5. Differentiate between apoptosis and necrosis, including their causes, cellular changes, and inflammatory responses.
6. Explain the stages of tooth development and the role of epithelial–mesenchymal interactions.
Level 2 — Core MDS Oral Pathology
Tests postgraduate depth, mechanisms, classification, and differential diagnosis.
7. Define oral leukoplakia. Discuss its clinical presentation, histopathology, and relationship to oral epithelial dysplasia.
8. Explain the histopathological features of oral squamous cell carcinoma, including evidence of invasion.
9. Differentiate oral lichen planus from oral lichenoid lesions based on clinical and histopathological findings.
10. Explain the etiopathogenesis, histopathology, and clinical significance of oral submucous fibrosis.
11. Compare odontogenic keratocyst and dentigerous cyst based on clinical, radiographic, and histopathological features.
12. Describe the histopathological variants of ameloblastoma and explain their diagnostic importance.
13. Differentiate pemphigus vulgaris and mucous membrane pemphigoid using histopathology and direct immunofluorescence.
14. Explain the pathogenesis of dental caries, including the role of microbial biofilms and the ecological plaque hypothesis.
Level 3 — Advanced Histopathology and Molecular Pathology
Tests deeper reasoning rather than simple retrieval of definitions.
15. Explain the cellular and architectural features used to assess oral epithelial dysplasia. Discuss the limitations of dysplasia grading in predicting malignant transformation.
16. Discuss the molecular mechanisms involved in oral squamous cell carcinogenesis, including the roles of TP53, EGFR, and cell-cycle dysregulation.
17. Differentiate pleomorphic adenoma and mucoepidermoid carcinoma with respect to histopathology and relevant diagnostic investigations.
18. Explain the immunopathogenesis of oral lichen planus, emphasizing the role of T lymphocytes and basal keratinocyte damage.
19. Discuss the role and limitations of immunohistochemistry in the diagnosis of oral potentially malignant disorders and oral squamous cell carcinoma.
20. Explain the pathogenesis of chronic periapical inflammation and differentiate periapical granuloma from radicular cyst histopathologically.
Level 4 — Clinical and Histopathological Case Reasoning
These are fictional postgraduate educational cases. Aster should give differential diagnoses and investigations rather than unwarranted certainty.
21. A 52-year-old patient has a persistent, non-scrapable white patch on the lateral border of the tongue. Discuss the differential diagnosis, histopathological possibilities, and appropriate diagnostic investigations.
22. A 45-year-old patient has a non-healing ulcer on the lateral tongue persisting for six weeks, with indurated margins. Discuss the possible pathological diagnoses and the histopathological findings that would help distinguish them.
23. A 25-year-old patient presents with a well-defined unilocular radiolucency around the crown of an impacted mandibular third molar. Discuss the differential diagnosis and the histopathological features needed for confirmation.
24. A biopsy from the buccal mucosa shows suprabasal epithelial clefting, acantholytic keratinocytes, and a tombstone appearance of basal cells. Identify the leading diagnosis, explain its pathogenesis, and describe the confirmatory investigation.
25. A 40-year-old patient presents with a slowly enlarging, painless palatal swelling. Discuss the differential diagnoses, relevant tissue origins, and histopathological findings that can distinguish the possibilities.
Level 5 — Test Aster's Conversation and Teaching Abilities
These questions specifically test the weaknesses identified in your audit: F06, F07, F20, and F21.
26. Viva test: "Act as an MDS Oral Pathology examiner. Ask me one challenging viva question on oral epithelial dysplasia, wait for my answer, evaluate it using a clear rubric, and then ask a follow-up question."
27. Comparison test: "Compare conventional oral squamous cell carcinoma and verrucous carcinoma in a table covering clinical features, histopathology, invasion patterns, and biological behavior."
28. Multi-part test: "Explain the pathogenesis of oral submucous fibrosis, compare it with oral lichen planus, and describe the histopathological findings of both conditions. Answer all three parts separately."
29. Progressive explanation test: "Explain the molecular mechanism of apoptosis in simple language." After Aster responds, send: "Now explain the intrinsic and extrinsic apoptotic pathways in postgraduate detail without repeating your earlier explanation."
30. Evidence and uncertainty test: "What does the current evidence say about the relationship between HPV infection and oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma? Distinguish oral cavity cancer from oropharyngeal cancer, provide verifiable sources, and explain the limitations of the evidence."
